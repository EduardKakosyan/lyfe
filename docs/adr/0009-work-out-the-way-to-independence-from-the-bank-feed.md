# Work out the way to financial independence from the bank feed and a plan the owner sets

Money showed where a month went and each account's balance. The owner wants what an investment account is in, the things someone keeping their own finances watches, the way to financial independence in particular, and pictures in place of bare figures. What people who do this keep track of was read up on first. It comes to a short list: what is kept of what comes in, a year's spending, net worth over time, the FI number and how far along to it, the years left, and what the investments are in; then the coast number, the money on hand as months of spending, and what the investments have grown by. lyfe shows all of those it can work out from what the banks already send, and nothing it would have to be told by hand or ask anyone new for.

**Holdings come from the bridge, and no prices are looked up.** SimpleFIN Bridge already lists what an investment account holds with every answer: how many of each, what they are worth as the bank last priced them, and sometimes what was paid. That is a day or two old, like everything in money (ADR 0007), and it is enough for looking at where one stands. A market data service would make the prices fresher at the cost of a second outside party and of sending it what the owner holds, which nothing does today. The list is not in the bridge's published format, so it is read for the little that is needed and nothing is taken for granted: a holding that cannot be read is counted and left out, and `lyfe-server money-check` says how many were read and what share of the account's balance they come to.

**Net worth is kept once a day, from the day this was installed.** The bank gives the balance now and no history. Every import keeps each account's balance for the day, and the line is drawn from those. The past could be rebuilt for a chequing account from its entries, but not for an investment account, whose balance moves with the market; a line rebuilt for some accounts and not others would jump on the day the rest joined it. So the line starts short and is true.

**What is kept is measured over months held whole.** The savings rate is what came in less what was spent, as a share of what came in, over the last twelve months lyfe holds from their first day, or as many as there are. A month is held from its first day only when every bank's history reaches back to it: banks give different amounts of history when first connected, and a month with one bank's spending and not the account the pay lands in would read as all spent and nothing in. The month still running is shown and not counted. It is the rate on pay as it lands in the bank: what is taken off before that, for tax or for a plan at work, is in neither figure, since the bank never sees it. That is the rate the sums below need, because they take spending to be what is not kept.

**What counts towards independence is what is set aside.** Not net worth: the money in the chequing account is for the month. The owner already marks savings as set aside, and an investment account starts that way from the first time its bank lists what it holds, so the owner decides what counts by the same mark. Savings set aside count as money on its way to being invested, less an emergency fund the owner sets in the plan: that part is kept for the unexpected and will not be invested. It is held back from the savings, never from the investments, and is still money on hand for the runway.

**The rest is worked out from a plan the owner sets, in today's money.** The FI number is a year's spending divided by the withdrawal rate. The years to it are how long what is set aside takes to grow there with a year's keeping added each year. The coast number is what would grow there by a given age with nothing more added. Growth is counted above inflation, so a figure twenty years off means what it would buy now. The plan starts careful rather than hopeful: 3.5% drawn a year and 4% growth. The well-known 4% held over thirty years in one country's history; over the fifty years and more that stopping early means, the studies that looked land between 3.25% and 3.5%. Financial planners' own guidelines for shares held for decades come to about 4% above inflation after fees; 5% is the long history and 7% is what the hopeful quote. A date that is wrong should be wrong by being late. The owner can change all of it, and lyfe never does, as with a food target. The owner's birth year is part of the plan, typed in the app and kept in the database like everything else of theirs; it is only for saying an age beside a year, and without it lyfe says years alone.

**lyfe shows; it does not advise.** It says what is held and how it has done, never what to buy, hold or sell.

## Consequences

- The date moves with the months it is worked from. One costly month, or one with three pay days, moves it by a year or more while few months are held; the screen says how many it is from. Twelve months steady it.
- A month counts as whole when every bank's oldest entry is on or before its first day, the rule the month's own comparison uses too (ADR 0007). The first rule took the oldest entry of any bank: with the card's bank giving six months and the pay's three, it counted three months with spending and nothing in, and put what was kept at a fifth of what it was. A bank connected later now shortens the months counted until its history fills in. A bank whose accounts rarely move can show its first entry a little after its history starts, and holds the months back by at most that. A bank with no entries at all, like a broker the bridge sends balances for and nothing else, says nothing about it.
- A month is not checked for holes. Each import re-reads the last two weeks (ADR 0007), so a bank that waited longer than that to be signed in to again, or a server that was down longer, leaves days that are never asked for; the month still counts as whole and reads low, and so does the date. The same goes, for a few days, for a month just ended whose last purchases have not posted. FIRE shows what is wrong with a bank at its top, like the month; going back for what was missed is not built.
- When a bank is connected later, net worth steps up on the day it is first seen. That is the same step a rebuilt past would have put in the line, and it cannot be drawn away without hiding money the owner has. The change said beside the figure leaves such an account out, so it is never read as a gain.
- The figures are as good as the sorting. Money in from a friend counts as income until the owner says it was money moved, and a card payment whose other half lyfe cannot see counts as spending until then.
- An investment account's balance is taken to be the whole account, holdings included, and what is in it and in no holding is shown as the difference. A bank that gave only the loose cash as the balance would make net worth too low; `money-check` shows the share the holdings come to, which would then be far over the whole.
- A bank that sends an empty list for an investment account is taken at its word: the account holds nothing and is money in the bank until the list comes back. If that was the bridge stumbling, the runway is too long until the next import.
- What was paid for a holding is the bank's word, and some banks do not give it. Growth is said only of the holdings that have it, and how many that is.
- Nothing is converted. A holding or an account in another currency is listed with its currency and left out of every total and every bar, as an account in another currency already was.
- What is not in the feed is not shown: the fees inside a fund, tax, pensions, and how much room a registered account has left. Those stay with the owner.
- Net worth before the day this was installed is not known and never will be.
- The plan's birth year is personal. It is in the database and so in the encrypted backup, and never in this repository, in settings or in logs.
