## What the first version is

Step 8 update (2026-11-23, partly built): Progress now includes pool distance, tape estimates, holds, gym weeks and weight. Pool and tape entry currently require a connection. The tape reminder is on Progress, not a push. The owner can set a weekly rate for their weight and confirm completed food days. A proposal requires three non-overlapping seven-day averages (at least three mornings each), two changes missing the weekly rate on the same side by more than 0.25 percentage points, and 14 confirmed days with known intake under unchanged settings. Approval changes calories by 100 from today and leaves protein alone. Its code came before the money update in this repository's history, so the installs from then on carried it to the Spark and the phone, but it has not been walked through on the phone, and the pool and tape entry screens are plain forms, not yet drawn in the Poster style. The rules are in [rules.md](rules.md#training-tape-and-target-review).

Money update (2026-11-23, built and tested on this Mac): money moved ahead of study once a provider was confirmed (ADR 0007). Accounts and posted transactions are imported from the banks through SimpleFIN Bridge by the backend itself, which replaces the trial script and its separate database. Money shows a month's spending across every account by category, with the entries behind it and the accounts; a card payment is paired with its other half and left out; an entry is sorted by the owner once for its payee; savings and investments are set aside. It was tried in the simulator against a local backend and a stand-in bridge: a month imported, entries sorted from the black bar, and the month redrawn. It is installed on the Spark, where the first real import brought back about six months of posted transactions in four requests, and on the phone. Its check on the real phone (sorting a month, and its figures set against the statements) is still to do. Bills, what is left until payday, what the investments hold and card rewards are not built.

Worth and FIRE update (2026-11-24, built and tested on this Mac, installed): Money has three sections, MONTH, WORTH and FIRE (ADR 0009). The import now also stores what each investment account holds, as the bridge lists it, and keeps every account's balance once a day. Worth shows net worth, the line it has drawn since it began being kept, what it is made of, the holdings with what they have grown by, and the accounts, which moved there from the month. FIRE shows what was kept of what came in over the months held whole, the FI number and the years to it, the coast number, the runway, and the plan it is worked out from, which the owner sets. It was tried in the simulator against a local backend and the stand-in bridge, which now has a broker: an import, Worth with its line, an account set aside from Worth, FIRE, and the plan changed and saved. It is installed on the Spark and the phone, together with study and goals below. Its checks there are still to do: what `money-check` says of the real investment accounts (how many holdings are read, and that they come to about the whole balance), net worth set against the banks' own figures, and the months FIRE counts set against the statements. The net worth line starts the day it was installed, 2026-11-24.

FIRE correction (2026-11-24, installed on the Spark and the phone): what was kept read far lower than it was. Banks give different amounts of history when first connected, and a month counted as whole once any bank reached its first day, so months counted with spending and nothing coming in. A month is now whole only once every bank reaches back to its first day, for FIRE and the month's comparison alike. The investment account imported before investment accounts were told apart is now set aside, as one is from the start, and the plan has an emergency fund, held back from the savings set aside and never from investments. On 2026-11-25 the owner's word on an account's currency was added, installed on the Spark and the phone: the bridge put a broker's account in another currency, with none on its holdings, which left it out of every total. Its check on the phone (FIRE and WORTH counting that account, and the holdings it lists) is still to do.

Study update (2026-11-24, built and tested on this Mac): goals and study were designed from research on goal pursuit, study and net-worth tracking (ADR 0010), and steps 11 to 15 were added. Step 11 is the Worth and FIRE update above, built alongside. Step 12 is built: study sessions are timed in the app on a subject and counted by the backend, with the week prompt. It was tried in the simulator against a local backend: a 92-minute session was recorded with its figures and Home showed the week. It is installed on the Spark and the phone; its check on the real phone (a week of study, one session timed with the phone locked) is still to do. Steps 13 and 14 are built and tested on this Mac, in the backend's tests and the simulator's galleries: goals on a breath hold, the weight trend, body fat and study hours, against their pace, three active, with their changes kept; and exams, with practice scores, readiness by the owner's rule and the exam's parts, and the result. ## How the work is cut

Each step below works end to end, from capture to a screen you can look at, before the next one starts. After any step you have something usable, and you can stop or reorder.

| 5 | Log your weighed breakfast and lunch with one tap and see target against actual | 1 |

## The steps

**1. Watch to Home screen.** The thinnest slice that proves the whole chain. Backend: a Rust service and PostgreSQL on the Spark, reachable only over Tailscale, with lyfe's own sign-in; a job that polls the Garmin Personal API and stores activities. App: a Swift shell with sign-in and the Home screen showing this week's gym squares. Check: do a strength session, sync the watch, and the square fills without touching lyfe.

**2. Streaks and recovery.** Backend: the streak rules as one tested module (weeks with four sessions, one miss recovered by completing the next week, two misses end the streak, previous best kept). Push notifications through Apple for "streak needs N sessions by Sunday", sent only when there is something to act on. App: the full gym band with numeral, squares and the striped recovery state. Check: replay your real Garmin history through the rules and confirm the weeks come out as you would count them.

**3. Breath session.** App: the full-screen timer, running fixed rounds with the screen locked, one tap to end a hold, working offline. Backend: stores every hold, counts weeks with three sessions, and sets the next table from the last session using your fixed-round method. App: the after-session screen. Check: run a session in airplane mode, reconnect, and see it counted once.

**4. Weigh-in.** A single morning notification answered by typing the number. Backend computes the 7-day average. App shows the weight line on Progress. Check: reply from the lock screen without opening the app.

**5. Saved meals and targets.** You define the usual breakfast and a batch lunch from weighed ingredients. App: the Food screen with target against actual, one-tap logging, the daily logging streak with recovery. Check: a full weekday logged in three taps.

**6. Photo and barcode meals.** Photo plus one line goes to gemma4:31b on the Spark, which returns items, grams, calories and protein. App shows the estimate with a range of about ±35%, lets you correct a portion, and asks about oil only for fried, sauced or restaurant meals. Barcode lookup against a downloaded food database. A meal is built from several of these and from foods typed by hand, added up as they are added, logged as one meal and saveable for one tap. Captured offline, estimated on reconnect. Check: the daily total shows measured and estimated separately.

**7. Meal illustrations.** A worker on the Spark runs FLUX.2 klein 4B on the photo and description, drawing the meal as it is with ink outlines and its dish in a Poster colour (ADR 0006), sets the background to the app's paper, and attaches it to the meal. It loads only when memory is free and falls back to the photo. Check: log a meal, and the illustration appears later without having delayed the log.

**8. Targets, pool, tape, Progress.** lyfe proposes a calorie target change only when weigh-ins are sufficient, the trend has been off for two weeks and logs are complete, and you approve it. One-number pool entry. Tape measurement every two weeks with a body-fat estimate. Progress screen with holds, gym weeks and weight.

**9. Mac and MCP.** A Mac app sharing the Swift code, and an MCP server exposing the same operations the apps use, with the same permission checks. This delivers the "usable by me and by an AI" boundary from discovery.

**10. Money.** Backend: a job that asks SimpleFIN Bridge for balances and posted transactions a few times a day and stores them under the bank's own ids; the rules for what an entry is (an expense, a refund, money in, money moved) as one tested module, with card payments paired and kept out of spending; the owner's word on a payee kept and applied to every entry from it. App: the Money screen with the month's figure, categories, entries and accounts, the sorting screen, and a strip on Home. Check: connect the banks, and a month's spending by category matches the statements, with no card payment counted.

**11. Worth and FIRE.** Backend: the import stores what each investment account holds and keeps every account's balance once a day; net worth and the holdings as one tested module, and the way to financial independence as another (what is kept over whole months, the FI number, the years to it, the coast number, the runway), worked out from a plan the owner sets. App: Money in three sections, with the net worth line, the holdings and what they have grown by, what came in and was spent a month at a time, the line ahead, and the plan's entry. Check: the holdings and net worth match the banks' own figures, and the months counted match the statements.

**12. Study sessions.** App: a timer on a subject, worked out from the clock so a locked phone or a closed app does not stop it, with pause, a start moved back up to two hours, and a stop by itself at three hours that asks when the owner really stopped; afterwards, what it was (questions, reading or a build), the questions answered and right first time, or a build's explain-back. Backend: a session counts once past 45 minutes, a day counts once, four days keep the week with the gym's recovery rule, builds count for at most two a week with an explain-back; the week prompt as for the gym. Check: a week of study on the phone, one session timed with the phone locked.

**13. Goals.** Backend: goals in three shapes (a figure to reach, a total to build up, a result to get), each worked out from what is already stored, with its pace and what would now be needed; three active, the rest parked; changes recorded. App: a Goals tab. Check: a goal on study hours moves as sessions are timed, and one on a hold as holds are recorded.

**14. Certifications.** A result to get, with the exam's published pass mark, domains and their weights, and how long it stays valid; practice scores typed after each test; readiness by a rule the owner sets; the expiry held once passed.

**15. Mock interviews.** Only after about twenty answers marked by the model on the Spark are set against the owner's own marking.

## Rules that apply to every step

- The backend decides streaks, progression and targets. The phone captures and displays.
- Every capture has a unique ID, so retries cannot double-count.
- Models on the Spark load on demand, check free memory first, cap their context and unload afterwards.
- No keys or tokens in the repository or in these notes.
- The repository is public. Nothing personal goes into it: no account or bank names, health figures, employer, location, or machine and device names. Research and discovery notes are kept outside it.
- The streak and progression rules get automated tests before any screen depends on them.

## Known risks

- The Garmin Personal API is new, has no published rate limits or cost, and could change.
- Photo estimates were about 35% off in the test; accuracy rests on the weighed meals and the weight trend.
- Illustration quality was judged on eight stock images; your own meals may behave differently.
- The Spark is shared, and nothing guarantees memory will be free when a model is needed.
